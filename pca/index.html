Why PCA is sensitive to measurement units

Rescaling feature by (say cm → mm, ) is with , so

is not similar to ( isn’t orthogonal), so its eigenvectors are genuinely different, not just rescaled. PCA maximizes variance in the numbers it is given. Make one feature’s numbers 1000× bigger and its variance is 106× bigger, so PC1 snaps onto that axis. That reflects the choice of units, not anything about the data. Try “mm” for height above.

The fix (Murphy §20.1.3.1): standardize each column, . The covariance of standardized data is the correlation matrix , , and doesn’t change under because each cancels between the numerator and . For two features , whose eigenvectors are always with eigenvalues , whatever the units.

When not to standardize: when all features share meaningful units (pixel intensities, the same sensor). There, a low-variance feature really is less informative, and standardizing would inflate its noise up to variance 1.

Raw vs. centered: the covariance already subtracts the mean, so the raw and centered views give the same and the same PCs; only the origin moves. PCA without centering (eigenvectors of on raw ) would point PC1 at the mean instead. The raw view draws that direction faintly from the origin so you can see it.

    The training curve is exactly (Eq. 20.26, using the sample eigenvalues). Test error usually sits above it because the training directions are estimates; with a small the gap is larger.

    Why there’s no U shape. In most models, test error eventually rises with complexity (overfitting). Here it can’t: the reconstruction of a test point is its orthogonal projection onto , and these spans are nested. Adding a direction can only bring the projection closer to the point, for any point. At the span is all of and the error is exactly 0. PCA reconstruction isn’t a likelihood: it never pays for using more parameters. So “minimize test reconstruction error” always says , and Murphy turns to scree plots, explained-variance thresholds and profile likelihood (Module 6). A probabilistic model (PPCA, §20.2) does show a U-shaped test log-likelihood.
