The Wrong Codebook

To send the weather in bits, you give short codewords to the weather you expect and long ones to the weather you don’t. That makes every belief a codebook. When your beliefs match reality, your messages are as short as possible: that length is the entropy. When they don’t, you pay extra on every symbol, and the extra is the KL divergence.

Things to try

  • Believe the truth. Copy reality into your belief. The red and green disappear and the waste drops to zero.
  • Rule out snow. It snows 20% of the time, but you expect it 1%. Every snowy day costs almost 7 bits.
  • Make the opposite mistake. Swap the roles: snow is rare and you over-expect it. You waste far less.
  • Shrug at everything. A uniform belief gives every codeword 2 bits. The waste is exactly 2 − H(p).
  • Nothing to compress. When reality is uniform, the entropy hits its 2-bit maximum.
