Manifold Learning Playground

High-dimensional data often has only a few real degrees of freedom. A photo has thousands of pixels, but the photos you actually see vary in a handful of ways. Manifold learning tries to recover those few coordinates: it finds a low-dimensional map for data , , that keeps the shape of the curved surface the data lies on.

Order of play: build intuition in sections 1–3, run the real algorithms in section 4, read the objectives in section 5, then check yourself in section 6. All the math on this page runs in your browser.

Understanding manifolds

A manifold of dimension is a space that looks like flat near every point, even if it is curved overall. The Earth looks flat from your yard and round from orbit.

Locally Euclidean

Near a point , the surface hugs its tangent plane . A Taylor expansion gives a gap of roughly , where is the bending, so . Shrink and watch it happen. This is the whole license for manifold learning: small neighborhoods can be treated as linear.

Globally curved

Grow and the plane stops fitting. On the sphere the gap grows without limit. No single flat chart covers it, and no map to keeps all distances. That is why world maps distort Greenland.

Riemannian structure

A Riemannian manifold adds an inner product on every tangent space: the metric . With it you can measure curve lengths, and the shortest curves are geodesics. The Swiss roll has : it is curved in but intrinsically flat, like rolled paper. So it can be unrolled with no stretching. The sphere () and the saddle () cannot.

Two choices simplify the textbook versions: kernel PCA uses only the RBF kernel, and t-SNE runs a fixed number of iterations from one seeded random start.
