Two Kinds of 50/50

Byld Blue’s sales inbox gets more replies than anyone can read. Two models guess whether a reply means “interested.” A human can label only a few replies. Both replies below come out at 50% interested once the two models are averaged. BALD (Bayesian Active Learning by Disagreement) says only one of them is worth a human label.

Check yourself

Both replies are 50/50 overall. Which should you spend a human label on, and why?

Label Reply 1. Its BALD is about 0.919 bits; Reply 2’s is 0.

In Reply 1 each model is nearly certain, but they are certain of opposite answers. Whatever the human says, one model gave it 99% and the other 1%, so the label settles which model to trust: the weights jump from 50/50 to 99/1.

In Reply 2 both models already say 50%. Either answer is exactly as likely under both, so the weights don’t move and nothing is learned about which model is better. The uncertainty is aleatoric (noise in the reply itself), not epistemic (disagreement between models). Plain entropy cannot tell these apart, because both replies have bit. BALD subtracts the part the models already expect to remain.
