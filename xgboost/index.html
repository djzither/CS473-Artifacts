Boosting Through the Highstorm

Dalinar wants to know how many highstorms each bridge on the Shattered Plains will survive before it has to be rebuilt. No single engineer forecasts it well. So he lets them take turns: each new engineer looks only at where the forecast so far went wrong and proposes a small correction. That is gradient boosting.

No spoilers beyond The Way of Kings.

The bridge ledger

Invented for teaching · not from the books

Twelve made-up bridges. Three things Dalinar’s engineers can measure, and one thing they want to predict.

Age
months since the bridge was built
Strength
timber and lashings, 1 (green softwood) to 10 (seasoned, bronze-banded)
Exposure
how squarely it faces the storm’s leading edge from the east, 1 to 10
Storms
highstorms it survived before it had to be rebuilt: the target

Machine-learning words, in plain English

prediction
the forecast so far: how many storms we think a bridge will last
loss
a penalty for a bad forecast. Here: half the squared miss
residual
what happened minus what we forecast. The miss, with a sign
gradient
the slope of the loss: which way, and how hard, to nudge a forecast to lower the penalty
hessian
how fast that slope changes: how quickly a nudge stops paying off
weak learner
one tiny decision tree, a couple of yes/no questions. One engineer
learning rate
how much of each correction we actually accept
regularization
deliberate caution: making the model pay for being bold or complicated
