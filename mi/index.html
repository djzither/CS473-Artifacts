Copies of Copies

A fair coin X is observed through a noisy channel to give Y. Then Y is processed, through more noise, into Z. Every step can only lose information about X, never create it. That is the data processing inequality, and the chain rule tells you exactly where the lost bits went.

Noise

Y is X, flipped with probability p.

Z is Y, flipped again with probability q. Z never looks at X.

Things to try

  • No information lost. p = 0.1, q = 0. Z is an exact copy of Y.
  • Some information lost. p = 0.1, q = 0.2. Part of the top bar turns red.
  • All information lost. p = 0.1, q = 0.5. Z is a fresh coin flip.

h₂(t) = −t log₂t − (1−t) log₂(1−t), the entropy of a coin that lands heads with probability t, with h₂(0) = h₂(1) = 0. All values are in bits.

Reading the notation

I(X;Y)
How much knowing Y tells us about X. The semicolon separates the two things being compared.
I(X;Y,Z)
How much knowing Y and Z together tells us about X. The comma bundles them into one observation.
I(X;Y|Z)
How much extra Y tells us about X when Z is already known. The vertical bar means “given.”
I(X;Z|Y)
How much extra Z tells us once Y is known. Here it is 0: Z is made from Y alone, so it has nothing new to say.

Why the bars add up

The chain rule splits what Y and Z tell us together into “first one, then the extra from the other.” You can peel them off in either order:

I(X;Y,Z) = I(X;Y) + I(X;Z|Y)

I(X;Y,Z) = I(X;Z) + I(X;Y|Z)

In the first line, I(X;Z|Y) = 0, so I(X;Y,Z) = I(X;Y): once you hold Y, also holding Z adds nothing. Setting the two lines equal gives

I(X;Y) = I(X;Z) + I(X;Y|Z)

which is exactly the picture: the top bar is the other two laid end to end.

Processing can’t help

Mutual information is never negative, so I(X;Y|Z) ≥ 0. The identity then gives the data processing inequality:

I(X;Z) ≤ I(X;Y)

You can see it in the formulas too: r − p = q(1 − 2p) ≥ 0, so Z is always at least as noisy as Y, and h₂ only grows on [0, ½].

  • q = 0: Z = Y, nothing is lost, and I(X;Y|Z) = 0. Equality holds.
  • q = 0.5: Z is a fair coin no matter what Y is, so Z is independent of X and I(X;Z) = 0. All of I(X;Y) moves into the red bar.
  • Any q: no cleverness in the processing step can push I(X;Z) above I(X;Y). Processing can keep information or destroy it, never create it.
